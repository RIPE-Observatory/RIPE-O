Introduction back to ToC

Research integrity assessments bring together heterogeneous evidence and judgement. A single assessment may involve publication metadata, registration records, post-publication notices, comments from the research community, information extracted from the work itself, and outputs produced by human reviewers or automated systems. The final outcome is therefore only one part of the record. To make an assessment auditable, reproducible, and reusable, the provenance of the assessment process also needs to be represented. The Research Integrity Provenance and Evidence Ontology (RIPE-O) provides this vocabulary. It represents a research integrity assessment as a case in which a work is assessed, integrity-related questions are investigated, evidence is considered, and hypotheses or answers are produced. Its modelled concepts remain generic: works, questions, hypotheses, evidence, activities, and agents. RIPE-O is designed as a focused extension of established ontologies rather than as a replacement for them. It builds on TIDO for decision traces, PROV-O for provenance, and FaBiO for works and publication notices. RIPE-O adds the research-integrity-specific terms needed to describe assessment cases, integrity evidence, registry evidence, study-design evidence, publication notices, reviewers, automated agents, and assessment outcomes.
